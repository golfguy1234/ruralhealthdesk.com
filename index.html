Rural Health Desk

Justin Brilliant

This summer I started helping a dermatology health-tech company work through the CMS Rural Health Transformation Program (RHTP). Making sense of the program meant building a registry of its money across all 50 states. It records who has been awarded funding, how much, and what each award is for, in the states' own words. That registry became the Rural Health Desk, and keeping it is now my full-time work.

As of 2026-08-25 it holds 1,007 awards across the 21 states that have published awards so far. 791 of them carry a published dollar amount, and those total $815,564,069. Every award figure traces to a public document.

The total counts awards only. It has moved down four times since I first assembled it, by $151,245,497 in all, as rows turned out to be announcements, applications, or in one case a carve-out I had accepted on a premise I could not source. I will send the dated list of all four revisions to anyone who asks.

The other 29 states have not published their first award lists yet. The program runs five years, and I read all 50 states and add each list as it lands.

Two examples

Kansas awarded Gove County Medical Center $3,000,000 under the REH Conversion/Transformative Capital Investment Grant Program (REH/CAP):

“Replacement of an unreliable mobile MRI with a permanent fixed MRI system, restoring reliable diagnostic imaging access for this 21-bed critical access hospital (CAH) in western Kansas.”

New Jersey awarded Inspira Medical Centers, Inc. $1,460,000 for its Elmer site under its Building Rural Hospital Capacity program:

“The project centers on implementing an end-to-end virtual nursing model with a centralized command center, virtual observation, and standardized care coordination workflows, to preserve access, and stabilize staffing.”

The wording is the states’ own, and both award lists are public: the Kansas award document and the New Jersey award document.

For companies that sell into rural health, I find the awards whose language matches what they sell, and then do the work that follows: the research, the outreach, and, where a program is still open, the application itself. For one rural health organization I wrote the entire application in under 24 hours.
