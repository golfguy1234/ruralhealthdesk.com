How it works

The awards. The CMS Rural Health Transformation Program is putting fifty billion dollars into rural health over five years, and the money flows through the states. Every state runs its own program, publishes its own documents, and announces its own winners on its own schedule. The Rural Health Atlas reads those documents and records who won, what the award pays for, and the amount when the state publishes one.

The organizations. The Atlas covers more than 30,000 healthcare organizations and sites across all fifty states, from critical access hospitals and rural health clinics to EMS agencies, public health departments and tribal health entities. The classes come from the states' own eligibility words.

The vendors. The Atlas also tracks close to 1,000 healthcare companies selling into rural health. It reads the scope of the published awards and matches them to the categories of companies that sell what the award is meant to buy. The result is a ranked set of funded organizations whose awards relate to what you sell, with the published award behind every match.
