Where is the rural health transformation money actually going?

I read what each state publishes and keep a verified registry of every funded award the states have published under the CMS Rural Health Transformation Program (RHTP): who was awarded money, how much, and what each award is for, in the states’ own words.

Every figure traces to a public state document. Data as of August 25, 2026. 791 of the 1,007 awards carry a published dollar amount; the total counts only those. The awards include state allocations, pass-throughs, and state procurements as well as awards to care organizations.

I track one program: the CMS Rural Health Transformation Program, which runs across 50 states over five years. I do not track all federal funding, and I do not track all rural health money. That narrowness is what makes every number on this site checkable.

As of August 25, 2026, 21 states had published their first award lists, and the other 29 had not. I read all 50 and add each list as it lands.

Two awards, worked completely

Kansas, $3,000,000. “Replacement of an unreliable mobile MRI with a permanent fixed MRI system, restoring reliable diagnostic imaging access for this 21-bed critical access hospital (CAH) in western Kansas.”

The scope sentence names the purchase: a fixed MRI system for a 21-bed hospital. That is what a match looks like when it is real. Read the full walkthrough.

New Jersey, $1,460,000. “[Elmer] The project centers on implementing an end-to-end virtual nursing model with a centralized command center, virtual observation, and standardized care coordination workflows, to preserve access, and stabilize staffing.”

The scope names an end-to-end virtual nursing model. That is a category match, written in the state’s own words. Read the full walkthrough.

What an engagement looks like

For companies that sell into rural health, I find the funded awards whose published language matches what they sell. From there I do the work that follows: the research, the outreach support, and, where a program is still open, the application itself. For one rural health organization I wrote the entire application in under 24 hours.
