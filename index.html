The catch

Every state runs its own version of this.

CMS is putting $10 billion a year into rural health for five years, $50 billion in all, and the money goes out through the states. Each state writes its own plan and announces its own winners on its own schedule.

If you've gone looking for this money already, you've probably hit the same wall I did... it's confusing. One state posts a clean spreadsheet, the next buries its awards in a PDF appendix. I never found one place that had all of it.

Rural Health Atlas

So I built one.

This is not a login and a spreadsheet. The Atlas is the database I built for my own work, and everything below runs on it.

Awards

I read the awards as they're published.

I keep a dated copy of the document behind each one. Every row names the organization that received it, and the amount when a state discloses one. Some states don't.

Organizations

Every award has an organization behind it.

A name in a state PDF isn't much on its own, so the Atlas ties recipients to the national roster I keep of critical access hospitals, health centers, rural health clinics, EMS agencies, health departments and tribal health entities, among others.

Vendors

Then I rank the fit.

The Atlas also tracks the companies selling into this work, in areas including workforce, behavioral health, health IT, remote patient monitoring, telehealth, imaging and diagnostics, and EMS. I use the scope text of the awards to rank which of them fit. The ranking is mine.

Here's one row. Kansas awarded Gove County Medical Center $3,000,000 for "Replacement of an unreliable mobile MRI with a permanent fixed MRI system, restoring reliable diagnostic imaging access for this 21-bed critical access hospital (CAH) in western Kansas." I filed it under imaging and diagnostics, straight from the state's own document.
