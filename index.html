How the Desk works

The Desk is three databases, and I keep them linked and current.

The awards. I record every funded award the states publish under the program, reading it from the state’s own document in the state’s own words, and I re-read it as the states move. The watch checks the fastest-moving state pages every hour through the day and the rest once a day, so a new award list surfaces within hours of going up. I then read it into the registry from the state’s own document.

The organizations. I keep a national database of rural health organizations, covering critical access hospitals, rural hospitals, rural health clinics, federally qualified health centers and their sites, EMS agencies, local public health agencies, community mental health centers, certified community behavioral health clinics, and tribal providers. Where the state names the recipient, the award is linked to the organization behind it.

The vendors. I keep a database of companies that sell into rural health, sorted by category, and I map each category against the funded scope text the states publish. That mapping is the ranking: for a vendor in a category I track, it shows which funded projects sit nearest to what it sells, in which states, and the organization behind each one.

Who I am

I am Justin Brilliant. I built the Desk this summer while helping a health-tech company work through the program and finding that nobody could say where the money was actually going. Reading every state’s documents, one at a time, became the registry. That registry became the Rural Health Desk, and keeping it is now my full-time work.

I track one program: the CMS Rural Health Transformation Program. I do not track all federal funding, and I do not track all rural health money. That narrowness is what makes the registry checkable.

I am paid by the companies I work for, never by the organizations receiving awards.
