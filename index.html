How the Desk works

The Desk is three linked databases: the awards, the organizations, and the vendors.

I record the funded awards the states publish under the program, reading each from the document that published it. A script checks the fastest-moving state pages every hour through the day and the rest once a day.

I keep a national database of rural health organizations. It covers:

  • critical access hospitals
  • rural hospitals
  • rural health clinics
  • federally qualified health centers and their sites
  • EMS agencies
  • local public health agencies
  • community mental health centers
  • certified community behavioral health clinics
  • tribal providers

I match the awards to the organizations behind them where the documents prove the match, and I record the organization the award names where they do not.

I keep a database of companies that sell into rural health, sorted by category, and I map the categories I track against the funded scope text. That mapping is the ranking. For a vendor in a category I track, the ranking shows which funded projects sit nearest to what that vendor sells.

Who I am

I am Justin Brilliant. I built the registry this summer while helping a health-tech company through the program, when nobody could say where the money was actually going. That registry became the Rural Health Desk, and keeping it is now my full-time work.

I track one program: the CMS Rural Health Transformation Program. I do not track all federal funding, and I do not track all rural health money. That narrowness is what makes the registry checkable.

I am paid by the companies I work for, never by the organizations receiving awards.
