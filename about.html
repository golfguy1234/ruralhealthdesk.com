About

I am Justin Brilliant. This summer I started helping a dermatology health-tech company work through the CMS Rural Health Transformation Program, and I hit a wall almost immediately: the money was real, but nobody could tell me where it was actually going. Press releases rounded, aggregators lagged, and the answers I needed were sitting in state documents nobody had read end to end.

So I built the thing I needed. One document at a time, state by state, the registry grew into a verified record of every funded award the states have published. That registry became the Rural Health Desk, and keeping it is now my full-time work. I read the states every day, and new award lists are verified and added as fast as I can read the documents behind them.

I track one program: the CMS Rural Health Transformation Program. I do not track all federal funding, and I do not track all rural health money. That narrowness is what makes the registry checkable.

What an engagement looks like

For companies that sell into rural health, I find the funded awards whose published language matches what they sell. From there I do the work that follows: the research, the outreach support, and, where a program is still open, the application itself. For one rural health organization I wrote the entire application in under 24 hours.

How I am paid

I work independently. I am paid flat fees by the vendors I work for, and those fees never depend on an award landing. When my work touches both sides of the same program, I disclose it to both sides. Organizations receiving the grants never pay me.
