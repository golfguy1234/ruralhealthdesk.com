A $3,000,000 MRI in Gove County, Kansas

Registry capture date: August 4, 2026. All quotes are verbatim from the document linked below.

What the state published

Kansas awarded Gove County Medical Center $3,000,000 under its 2026 Rural Emergency Hospital Conversion and Transformative Capital Investment Program (REH/CAP):

“Replacement of an unreliable mobile MRI with a permanent fixed MRI system, restoring reliable diagnostic imaging access for this 21-bed critical access hospital (CAH) in western Kansas.”

The wording is the state’s own, from the Kansas award document. The document carries the state’s own program name rather than the federal label; REH/CAP is one of the initiatives Kansas funds from its Rural Health Transformation Program allocation, which is how the registry classifies it.

What the language means

The sentence is unusually concrete for a grant document. The state is not funding a study, a plan, or a pilot: it is buying a machine and the access that machine restores. A critical access hospital (CAH) is a small rural hospital, 25 beds or fewer, that Medicare pays under special rules to keep it open. The document does not say what an unreliable MRI costs patients here, so I will not claim that, but restoring imaging access is the stated point of the award.

It is worth reading what the sentence does not say. It does not name a vendor. It does not give a timeline. It does not say whether installation, service, or reading the images is bundled into the figure. I do not know those things from this document, and the registry does not claim them.

How the match was made

The registry stores the state’s sentence verbatim and matches vendor categories against the words the state chose. “MRI system” and “diagnostic imaging access” put this award in imaging and diagnostics. The match came from the document’s own words, not from a guess about what a hospital like this probably needs.
